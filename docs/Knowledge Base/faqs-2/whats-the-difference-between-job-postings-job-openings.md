Job postings and job openings both help assess labor-market demand, but they measure different aspects of that demand. In many cases, the true level of demand for a role falls somewhere between these two indicators.

## Job Postings

Job postings reflect the maximum potential demand for a role but only when employers actively advertise online. These postings are voluntarily published by companies to attract applicants. Because posting is optional, some jobs appear frequently in job postings, while others are rarely advertised even if they are in high demand.

Although postings do not provide a complete picture of demand, they offer rich detail about the job, including

* Desired skills
* Detailed job titles
* Employer information
* Specific responsibilities or requirements

Since one job posting does not necessarily represent one actual job opening, and because coverage varies widely by occupation, postings serve best as a source of granular job detail, not a definitive measure of labor demand.

## Job Openings

Job openings provide a more conservative estimate of demand by accounting for both expected job growth and the need to replace workers who leave their occupations. These estimates are produced using occupational replacement rates developed by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS).

BLS considers workers who

* Retire
*  Permanently leave an occupation
* Change careers
* Leave the workforce

However, the BLS methodology does not account for workers who move out of a region or switch jobs within the same occupation. Because of this, job openings measure demand more narrowly than postings, but they reflect a more structured and stable assessment of true workforce needs.
