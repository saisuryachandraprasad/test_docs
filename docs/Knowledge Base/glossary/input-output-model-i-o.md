An Input-Output (I-O) model describes how money moves through an economy by illustrating how industries buy from and sell to one another within a specific region. Beyond industry-to-industry transactions, an I-O model also incorporates government spending, household spending, investments, imports, and exports. Together, these components create a comprehensive picture of how a regional economy functions and how different sectors are interconnected.

**Why Input-Output Models Matter**

I-O models are valuable because they help explain not only what an economy produces, but how industries rely on each other to produce it. They support three key use cases:

**1. Measuring Economic Change**

An I-O model estimates how job gains or losses ripple through a regional economy. For example, when a business adds jobs, the model shows how many additional jobs may be created in supporting industries, how earnings may increase, and how regional sales may be affected.

**2. Understanding the Supply Chain**

I-O models can reveal a region’s supply chain by identifying which industries purchase goods and services locally versus outside the region. This insight is particularly useful for economic development organizations aiming to strengthen local suppliers, reduce leakage, and increase in region purchasing.

**3. Identifying Key Industries**

A region’s most important industries are not always the ones with the highest employment, such as retail or healthcare. An I-O model highlights industries that generate unusually strong economic impact such as advanced manufacturing, technology, or logistics by fueling growth in connected sectors. These industries often play a strategic role in regional development.
