The UK Standard Occupational Classification (UKSOC) is a hierarchical system developed by the UK Office for National Statistics (ONS) to classify and categorize occupations across the UK labor market.

## Taxonomy Structure

The UKSOC 2020 system consists of four hierarchical levels, each providing increasing detail

1. Major Group (1 digit): 9 groups
2. Sub-major Group (2 digits): 26 groups
3. Minor Group (3 digits): 104 groups
4. Unit Group (4 digits): 412 groups

**Hierarchy**: Major Group -> Sub-major Group -> Minor Group -> Unit Group

**Example Classification**

* Major group: Managers and directors
* Sub-major group: Business, research, and administrative professionals
* Minor group: Business, finance, and related associate professionals
* Unit group: Actuaries, economists, and statisticians

## Development of the Taxonomy

The UKSOC system was first introduced in 1990 and has undergone periodic revisions, with UKSOC 2020 being the most recent. The update process included

* Consultation with employers, trade unions, professional bodies, and government agencies
* Review of changes in the UK labor market
* Inclusion of new and emerging occupations

## Relationship to ISCO

UKSOC is aligned with the International Standard Classification of Occupations (ISCO-08), sharing the same four-level structure. However, UKSOC includes additional detail specific to the UK labor market, such as granular classifications for health and social care occupations that are not present in ISCO.

**Uses of UKSOC**

UKSOC supports decision-making across many sectors

* Employers use it for workforce planning and job design
* Policymakers rely on it for labor-market strategy and skills development
* Educators and training providers use it for curriculum and program alignment
* Researchers use it to analyze employment, wages, occupational trends, and more

Government agencies also use UKSOC to monitor and evaluate labor-market programs.

## Data Published by the ONS

The Office for National Statistics publishes a range of data tied to UKSOC classifications, including

* Employment and wage statistics
* Occupational projections
* Job openings and labor turnover data

UKSOC codes are also used to classify responses in surveys such as the Labour Force Survey, enabling detailed analysis of employment, unemployment, and topics like the gender pay gap by occupation and industry.
