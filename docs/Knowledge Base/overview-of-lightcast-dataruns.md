## Data

Lightcast collects and integrates labor market information from a wide range of sources. The three primary inputs are:

* Job postings published by employers ---- cross link TBD
* Professional career profiles ---- cross link TBD
* Government and private-sector datasets ---- cross link TBD

Using a suite of ***taxonomies*** to organize and align these sources, Lightcast builds a comprehensive picture of labor market activity worldwide. This information is delivered through an international dataset (Global) and three national datasets (United States, United Kingdom, and Canada).  ---- cross link TBD

Maintaining this global view is challenging because data sources and taxonomies update on different schedules particularly across countries. Job postings and profiles can refresh frequently, while government data depends on update timelines set by various agencies and programs.

## Dataruns

To ensure customers always receive the most current data available, Lightcast establishes a regular update cadence for all government sourced datasets and taxonomies. Each scheduled update is referred to as a datarun.

**Datarun Cadence**

* Global: 4 times per year ---- cross link TBD
* United States: 4 times per year ---- cross link TBD
* United Kingdom: 2 times per year ---- cross link TBD
* Canada: 2 times per year ---- cross link TBD

Each datarun is released first as a beta version, which customers may choose to explore. Approximately one week later, Lightcast releases the final version, which then becomes the default datarun for all users.

Within Lightcast products, users can access the current datarun as well as the two previous versions to help ease the transition between updates.

## Key Considerations

**Dataruns Affect Government Data Only**

Dataruns update the government sourced components of Lightcast datasets. They do not change job posting or profile data, except in rare cases where a new government taxonomy is introduced that alters how postings and profiles can be classified or searched.

**Each Datarun Is Unique**

Available data is not uniform across countries. For example, demographic data is updated more frequently in the United States than in other regions. An update to U.S. demographic information does not imply a corresponding update in UK or Canadian data.

**Local** vs **Global Taxonomies**

Each national dataset uses taxonomies specific to its region such as local occupation, industry, or education classifications. The Global dataset, however, applies a unified set of taxonomies to support international comparison.
