## What Are DDN Skills?

DDN Skills are occupation specific skill categories that describe the relationship between a skill and a given occupation. While common, specialized, and software skills describe what a skill is, DDN categories describe how essential or differentiating that skill is within an occupation.

The framework is structured as a hierarchy from foundational skills required for entry level competence to advanced skills associated with more specialized or senior roles.

**Occupational Skill Categories**

* **Necessary Skills**: These are the specialized skills foundational to an occupation and relevant across similar roles. They represent core capabilities required within the broader career area.
* **Defining Skills**: Defining skills represent the specialized skills most closely tied to the day-to-day tasks and responsibilities of the occupation.
* **Distinguishing Skills**: These advanced skills are called on less frequently but differentiate more specialized or senior workers. Individuals with distinguishing skills often stand out in the labor market.

## Where Can I Find DDN Skills?

The DDN Skills framework is available in the Occupation Overview/Snapshot reports in both Analyst and Developer. Because these reports require at least one occupation as input, they serve as a natural starting point for exploring DDN Skills. Lightcast will continue expanding the use and placement of DDN Skills across Analyst and Developer in future releases.

## How Can DDN Skills Help Me?

DDN Skills enable users to move beyond simple recall rates (e.g., which skills appear most often in postings) and instead understand which skills truly define an occupation and how workers differentiate themselves.

For example, rather than repeatedly seeing **communication** as a top skill, DDN clarifies whether communication is necessary but not sufficient for success in a role.

## Use Cases

* Support job seekers by helping them highlight essential and differentiating skills on their resumes.
* Improve curriculum design by aligning coursework with in-demand skills.
* Build core programs around Necessary Skills.
* Create advanced learning tracks around Defining and Distinguishing Skills.
* Increase student and worker marketability by teaching skills that help individuals stand out.
* Support salary growth by identifying skills associated with higher earnings (salary premium skills).
