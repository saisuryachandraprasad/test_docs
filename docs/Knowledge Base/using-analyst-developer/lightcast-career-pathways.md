## Overview

Career Pathways in the Lightcast Analyst platform provides an interactive view of how jobs relate to each other. It shows potential career paths, opportunities for advancement, and the skills needed to transition between roles.

## How Career Pathways Works

**Focus Occupation**

Users begin by selecting a Focus Occupation. The tool then displays a visual map showing related occupations based on skills found in job postings.

<Image border={false} />

**Comparison Occupation**

Users can choose a Comparison Occupation from the chart to explore

* How the two roles are connected
* Skill gaps between the roles
* Possible career transitions

This helps identify practical next steps or alternative talent pools.

<Image border={false} />

## Career Path Types

Career Pathways organizes transitions into four types to make the analysis easy to understand

| Type                | Meaning                                             |
| :------------------ | :-------------------------------------------------- |
| Advancement         | Higher pay, same occupation group as the focus role |
| Lateral Advancement | Higher pay, different occupation group              |
| Similar             | Comparable pay, same occupation group               |
| Lateral Transition  | Comparable pay, different occupation group          |

Occupations are also ranked using **Relevance Scores**, which measure how closely the skills of the comparison occupation align with the focus occupation.

**Identifying Opportunity Jobs**

Career Pathways helps users explore two major categories of related occupations

**Next-Step Jobs**

Roles a worker can move into from the focus occupation.

_Example_ A workforce agency helping job seekers understand which skills they need to advance—or helping employers identify internal skill gaps and build training programs.

**Feeder Jobs**

Roles that commonly feed talent into the focus occupation.

_Example_: A company hiring Business Development Specialists can find alternative talent pools with similar skills, widening their recruitment strategy.

## Skill Insights

Career Pathways provides deeper skill-level analysis to support transitions between occupations.

**Transitional Skills**

These are the most important skills needed to move from the focus occupation to the comparison occupation.
They are ranked by an Importance Score, highlighting where workers should focus their upskilling efforts.

_Example_: Transitioning from Market Research Analyst to Marketing Manager highlights the growing importance of strategic and leadership skills.

## Skill Gaps

Skill gaps compare the frequency of skills mentioned in postings for both occupations. They show differences in employer demand but may not represent all skills required for the transition.

## Why Career Pathways Matters

Career Pathways gives a detailed picture of how jobs connect across the labor market. Organizations can use it to

* Identify career advancement opportunities
* Build skill-based training programs
* Expand recruitment pipelines
* Support workforce planning and development strategies
