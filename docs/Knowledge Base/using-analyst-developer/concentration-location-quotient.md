## Overview

A Location Quotient (LQ) measures how concentrated a particular characteristic is in a region compared to the nation. LQs can be calculated for industries, occupations, resident demographics, online profiles, or job postings.

An LQ highlights what makes a region unique by showing whether a characteristic represents a larger or smaller share of local activity than the national average.

## Examples

**Industry Example**

If leather products manufacturing accounts for 2% of jobs in a region and 1% nationally, the LQ is `0.02 / 0.01 = 2.0`

An LQ of 2.0 means the industry is twice as concentrated locally as in the nation. Many economists consider an industry *concentrated* when its LQ is above 1.2.

**Occupation**

If registered nurses represent 10% of regional employment and 9% nationally, the LQ is `0.10 / 0.09 = 1.11` The occupation is slightly more concentrated locally than in the nation overall.

**Job Posting**

If software developer postings make up 3.9% of postings in Boise, ID, and 2.8% nationally, the LQ is `0.039 / 0.028 = 1.4` Demand for software developers is 1.4× more concentrated in Boise than in the nation.

**Online Profile**

If actors and comedians represent 4% of online profiles in Vancouver and 1% nationally, the LQ is `0.04 / 0.01 = 4.0` This means the region has four times the concentration of those occupations relative to the national average.

## Calculation

An LQ can be calculated for any regional characteristic that can be expressed as a percentage. Although national comparison is most common, any larger reference area such as a state, group of states, or a custom region can be used.

**Technical Definition**

LQ measures the relative concentration of an asset in a region versus a larger reference region. Let

* X = amount of the asset in the region
* Y = total amount of comparable assets in the region
* X' = amount of the asset in the reference region
* Y' = total comparable assets in the reference region

Then:

Regional share: `X / Y`

Reference share: `X' / Y'`

LQ: `(X / Y) ÷ (X' / Y')`

## Conclusion

Location Quotient is a powerful metric that highlights regional specialties by comparing local characteristics to national averages. Because the term **LQ** can be ambiguous, Lightcast refers to LQs in Analyst and Developer as specified concentrations, such as

* Employment Concentration
* Demographic Concentration
* Profile Concentration
* Posting Concentration
