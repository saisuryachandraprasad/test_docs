The Job Posting Analytics report helps you understand employer demand for specific roles and skills in any region. It shows the number of job postings, the types of jobs employers need, who is hiring, the skills and qualifications requested, and how intense demand is across local labor markets.

<Callout icon="💡">
  This article helps you quickly find answers to questions such as

  * How many postings are active?
  * What roles are in demand?
  * Who is hiring?
  * How intense is the hiring activity?
  * What job titles do employers use?
  * What qualifications do they expect?
  * What skills are they asking for?
</Callout>

## How to Use the Report

Start by choosing a region county, metro area (MSA), state, or national.

Run the report to view all postings in that region, or refine your results using filters such as occupation, job title, company, industry, skill, or keyword.

After you run the report, all filters appear in the sidebar so you can adjust the view at any time.

**Example**: Running the report for Physician Assistants in the Seattle MSA shows all hiring activity for that role in that region.

## Metrics Summary

The report shows two posting counts

* **Total postings**: All ads collected, including duplicates across sites.
* **Unique postings**: The true number of postings after removing duplicates.

For example, between September 2016 and August 2019, Seattle had 34,171 total postings and 6,542 unique postings for Physician Assistants.

**Posting Intensity**

Posting intensity is the ratio of total postings to unique postings.

A **3:1** intensity means each unique job is advertised three separate times often on different sites.

Higher intensity may indicate:

* An employer is urgently hiring
* A role is hard to fill
* Broader advertising is needed to reach candidates

The summary also shows median posting duration, the number of days a job ad typically stays open.
For Physician Assistants, postings stayed open for 36 days.

You will also see the number of employers competing for talent that matches your filters.

## Advertised Salary

The salary chart shows how many postings include pay information.

In the Seattle example:

Only 23% of postings listed salary

The median advertised salary was $134.7K, slightly above typical earnings for the area

## Sample Job Postings

The report includes sample full-text job postings used in the analysis.

This helps you to

* Verify context behind skill or qualification trends
* Review actual employer language
* See how posting content varies across sources

Click any sample to view the full posting.

The “Jump to Job Postings List” link opens the top 500 relevant postings.

<Image border={false} />

## Posting Trends Over Time

Trend charts display historical posting activity. You can view

* Monthly trends
* The most recent 30 days
* Year-over-year comparisons

Hovering over any point shows how many postings were active on that date in both the current year and the earliest

year in your selected range.

**Example**: On March 8, the report might show 176 postings in 2023 and 251 in 2022, a 30% decline.

A link in the corner allows you to open the detailed Job Postings Table.

<Image border={false} />

## Education & Experience Requirements

Following charts summarize

* Required education level
* Minimum education level
* Minimum years of experience

For example, 14% of Physician Assistant postings required a Master’s degree.

<Image border={false} />

## Postings vs Hires

This section compares monthly posting activity to actual hires from the Quarterly Workforce Indicators (QWI) dataset.

While postings show employer demand, hires show how many positions were actually filled. It’s common especially in

healthcare and IT for postings to exceed hires significantly.

**Example**: Employers posted nearly 300 PA jobs per month, but only about 40 were hired.

<Image border={false} />

## Who Is Hiring

This section highlights the top contributors to demand

* Top hiring companies
* Top cities posting
* Top mapped occupations
* Top job titles
* Top industries

For each company, the report shows

* Unique posting count
* Posting intensity
* Median posting duration

This helps you see which employers compete most for your selected talent.

<Image border={false} />

## In-Demand Skills

The Skills section compares the frequency of skills in postings with the frequency of those same skills in workforce profiles.

This helps to identify

* Skill shortages (high employer demand, low supply)
* Skill surpluses (lower employer demand, high supply)

**Example**: Nearly 20% of employers requested Radiology experience for Physician Assistants, but fewer than 5% of profiles listed it indicating a shortage.

<Image border={false} />

## Key Qualifications

This chart displays the most common certifications requested in postings.

For Physician Assistants, Nurse Practitioner credentials appear most often, even compared to other qualifications.

Qualifications represent third-party credentials such as licenses or professional certifications that validate specific skills or

competencies.
