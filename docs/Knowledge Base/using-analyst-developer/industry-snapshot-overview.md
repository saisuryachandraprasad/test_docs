The Industry Snapshot report provides a detailed overview of a selected industry, defined by its NAICS code. It summarizes jobs, occupational composition, trends, growth, demographics, demand, and more.

## Getting Started

To run an Industry Snapshot report

* Select one or more industries. You can search by industry name or NAICS code.
* Select a region (ZIP, city, county, MSA, state, or United States).

In this example, we analyze the Hospital industry within the Seattle–Tacoma–Bellevue, WA MSA.

<Image border={false} />

> Your region selection determines what data is available. To access the most complete dataset, choose a metropolitan statistical area (MSA).

<Image border={false} />

At the top of the report, a map highlights job intensity by county. Hover over each area to view the exact number of jobs in the selected industry.

## Executive Summary

<Image border={false} />

The first chart displays the supply of workers in the selected region compared to the national average, adjusted for regional size. For example, the Seattle MSA has nearly 50,000 hospital workers, indicating a lower-than-expected supply relative to national norms.

Earnings Per Job compares regional earnings to the national average for the same industry. Earnings are calculated as total pre-tax industry earnings divided by same-year industry employment. Only labor-related personal income is included.

The Demand (Job Postings) summary highlights employer demand for talent in the region.

## Supply (Jobs)

<Image border={false} />

This chart compares actual vs. expected employment to help evaluate whether supply is a strength or weakness for the region. A typical region of similar size would have 72,024 hospital employees, but the Seattle MSA has 49,991. This lower supply may make recruiting more challenging. The employment gap is expected to remain stable over the next five years.

## Demographic Overview

<Image border={false} />

This section provides diversity benchmarks for the selected industry and region. Each measure is compared to similarly sized areas nationwide.

* **Retiring Soon**: Workers ages 55 and older
* **Racial Diversity**: Number of employees from racially diverse groups
* **Gender Diversity**: Gender distribution in the industry

*Availability*: County, MSA, state, and national levels
*Not available*: City, ZIP code, census tract

## Industry Gain and Drain

Gain and Drain reflect the directional flow of workers moving into and out of the industry, based on online profiles. In this example, the inflow (Gain) and outflow (Drain) patterns are relatively aligned, though this is not always the case.

Results are available only at the national level and for 6-digit NAICS industries. Selections are limited to a maximum of 100 industries.

## Skills

<Image border={false} />

## Gross Regional Product (GRP)

Gross Regional Product (GRP) represents the region’s total economic output—including industry earnings, taxes on production and imports, and profits, minus subsidies. GRP reflects the entire region, not solely the selected industry.

In Washington

* Total industry earnings exceeded $6B in 2022
* Property income totaled $1.1B
* Tax revenue generated over $304.5M
* Combined, these components total $7.4B in GRP.
