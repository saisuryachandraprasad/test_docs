## Overview

Lightcast’s global data is designed to support accurate international comparisons. Similar to how the _**Lightcast Occupation Taxonomy**_ standardizes global occupation frameworks, Lightcast Administrative Areas (LAAs) provide a unified approach to representing local geographic divisions for each country. --- cross link TBD

The purpose of LAAs is to create a consistent geographic hierarchy that aligns with each country’s official administrative definitions. The table below references the local government equivalents used to build these standardized layers.

## FAQs

<Accordion title="What are Lightcast Administrative Areas?">
  Lightcast Administrative Areas (LAAs) are standardized geographic codes used across our global dataset. They align with local geocoding systems, such as the ***NUTS regions*** in the European Union or ***FIPS codes*** in the United States, enabling consistent statistical analysis across countries.   --------- cross link TBD
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why isn’t a particular metro area available in Global Metros?">
  Metropolitan definitions are based on official government classifications. To ensure usability and maintain a manageable set of regions, Lightcast applies minimum population thresholds that vary by country. Refer to the table above for specific criteria.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do global metros cover entire countries?">
  No. Global metros do not provide full national coverage. Smaller regions are included in national estimates but are not individually represented as metropolitan areas in the global tool.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How does Lightcast classify postings to geographic locations?">
  Lightcast collects thousands of job postings daily from around the world. Each posting is analyzed to identify a location reference, and one location is assigned per posting.
  The extracted location is then geocoded through Google services to obtain latitude and longitude. All other geographic fields such as city, county, state, and LAAs are derived from these coordinates.
</Accordion>
